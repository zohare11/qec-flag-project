# Phase 9 scientific scope

## Research questions

Phase 9 asks two questions left unresolved by Phase 8:

1. Do the physically certified repeated-round circuits actually have a vanishing first-order logical term and a nonzero second-order leading term when evaluated by explicit low-order fault analysis?
2. Can a detector-event decoder that reasons over sparse fault signatures approach or improve on the small-code history lookup without enumerating the complete history space?

## Physical model retained from Phase 8

- seven-qubit Steane code;
- three consecutive noisy syndrome-extraction rounds;
- Phase-7 bridge-routed, single-fault-certified physical circuits;
- explicit native CNOT Pauli faults;
- explicit persistent-data idling during every serialized preparation, native CNOT, and measurement interval;
- synthetic 12-node nearest-neighbour hardware graph and synthetic calibration families;
- no ideal recovery between noisy rounds;
- ideal final memory-boundary syndrome remains available to the decoder.

## Low-order expansion

For a selected physical circuit and context, Phase 9 evaluates the repeated-round single-fault catalog exactly.

- `C1` is the total weight of malignant one-fault outcomes.
- `C2` is the exact malignant two-fault coefficient when `C1=0`.
- The raw malignant three-fault weight is importance-sampled from distinct physical locations with proposal probability proportional to the product of physical fault weights.
- The reported third-order Taylor estimate subtracts the cubic contribution generated by the no-fault factors multiplying malignant pair events.

A negative estimated third-order coefficient is not a negative probability. Taylor coefficients beyond the leading term can be negative because of the expansion of the independent-location no-fault factors. The low-p probability remains dominated by the positive `C2 p^2` term when `C1=0`.

## Detector-hypergraph decoder

Measured syndrome histories are converted to detector events

`d_t = s_t XOR s_(t-1)`

with an all-zero initial boundary and the final ideal memory boundary included. Flag outcomes are appended as additional event bits.

Every modeled single physical fault defines a sparse hyperedge:

`(detector + flag event mask, resulting data Pauli, physical weight)`.

Equivalent single-fault signatures are aggregated. The decoder precomputes the best explanation for event patterns reachable using zero, one, or two aggregate hyperedges. Fault order is lexicographically primary: a known one-fault event pattern is never replaced by a two-fault explanation merely because the latter has a larger aggregate probability. This preserves the certified single-fault correction property under calibration shifts.

This is **not PyMatching or general MWPM**. Hyperedges can contain more than two detector events, and order-2 preprocessing grows polynomially only because the code/circuit family remains small. The decoder is a bridge between the exact lookup and a future scalable detector decoder, not the final decoder for a large code.

## Decoder baselines

Phase 9 compares:

- Phase-8 history lookup: exact over its modeled single-fault history table, with final-syndrome fallback for unseen multi-fault histories;
- Phase-9 order-2 detector-hypergraph decoder;
- temporal-majority syndrome decoding as a deliberately weak recognizable baseline.

## Claims Phase 9 can support

If the configured results reproduce the reference behavior, Phase 9 supports claims that:

- the selected certified circuits have `C1 = 0` under the three-round explicit fault model;
- their exact leading repeated-round logical coefficient is second order in the tested contexts;
- full syndrome/flag temporal information is essential relative to temporal-majority decoding;
- an order-2 detector-event decoder can exploit multi-fault structure that the single-fault history lookup does not explicitly enumerate.

## Claims Phase 9 does not support

Phase 9 does not establish:

- a threshold;
- device-level fault tolerance;
- a large-distance decoder;
- superiority to PyMatching/MWPM;
- realistic leakage/crosstalk behavior;
- real-backend performance;
- parallel syndrome scheduling;
- an ML or RL advantage.
